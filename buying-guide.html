Used semi truck buying guide

What to check, what to ask, and what to budget for before you buy a used Class 8 truck from anyone.

Mileage is the first number most buyers look at, and it is the least complete. Two trucks with the same odometer reading can have very different amounts of life left, depending on how they were run and maintained. Use this checklist to look past the odometer.

1. Start with the job

  • Sleeper or day cab. Over-the-road work needs a sleeper. Local and regional runs are usually cheaper to operate with a day cab.
  • Engine and horsepower. Match power to your freight and terrain. Heavy loads and hills need more than flat, light van freight.
  • Transmission. Automated manuals are easier to drive and to hire drivers for. Manuals are simpler to repair.
  • Rear axle ratio. A lower numeric ratio favors highway fuel economy. A higher one favors pulling power.

2. Ask for the engine computer report

The engine control module (ECM) records far more than the dash shows. Ask the seller to print the report and look at:

  • Engine hours and idle time. A truck that idled a large share of its life has more engine wear than its miles suggest.
  • ECM mileage. Compare it with the odometer. A large gap needs an explanation, such as a replaced cluster.
  • Fault codes. Look at active codes and at the history of repeated ones.
  • Average fuel economy. It tells you how the truck was driven and specified.

3. Check the emissions system closely

On modern diesels, aftertreatment repairs are among the most expensive. Ask about:

  • When the diesel particulate filter (DPF) was last cleaned or replaced
  • How often the truck regenerates, and whether forced regenerations have been needed
  • Any work on the EGR cooler or valve, SCR system, DEF pump or NOx sensors
  • Warning lights for the emissions system during your test drive

4. Engine and drivetrain

  • Look for oil, coolant and fuel leaks with the engine cold and again after it has run.
  • Check the coolant for oil and the oil for coolant.
  • Watch for heavy crankcase blow-by from the breather tube.
  • Listen for knocks at start-up and under load.
  • Ask for an oil analysis if the seller has one, or have a sample taken.
  • Shift through every gear. On automated transmissions, note any harsh or delayed shifts.
  • Check the clutch engagement point, driveshaft U-joints and differential seals.

5. Tires, brakes and suspension

  • Measure tread depth on every tire and look for uneven wear, which points to alignment or suspension problems.
  • Check that tires on the same axle match, and read the date codes.
  • Look at brake lining thickness, drums and air lines. Listen for air leaks with the system charged.
  • Inspect air bags, shocks, bushings and kingpins.
  • Check the fifth wheel for wear and for a jaw that locks firmly.

6. Frame, cab and sleeper

  • Sight down the frame rails for bends, cracks, heavy rust or repair welds.
  • Check cab mounts, door seals, and the floor for rust.
  • Test the heat, air conditioning, bunk heater and any auxiliary power unit.
  • Try every light, gauge, switch and the inverter.

7. Paperwork

  • Confirm the VIN on the title matches the VIN on the truck.
  • Ask whether the title is clear, and get a lien release if there was a loan.
  • Ask for maintenance records and the date of the last annual DOT inspection.
  • Get every promise in writing. A verbal promise is hard to enforce after the sale.
  • Know whether the truck is sold with a warranty or as-is, and read the terms.

8. Drive it, then get a second opinion

Drive the truck long enough to reach operating temperature. Watch the gauges, feel for vibration and pulling, and brake firmly at least once. Then have a diesel mechanic you trust look it over. A seller who is confident in the truck will not object.

9. Budget beyond the price

  • Sales tax, title and registration
  • Federal Heavy Vehicle Use Tax, filed on IRS Form 2290
  • Insurance, which your lender will require before the truck moves
  • A reserve for tires, brakes and the first repair, because every used truck will need one
This guide is general information to help you ask good questions. It does not replace an inspection by a qualified mechanic.
